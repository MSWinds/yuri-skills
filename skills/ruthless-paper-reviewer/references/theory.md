# Theory & conceptual papers

Use for papers whose core contribution is a proposition, model, or theory argued without
primary data — conceptual development, framework papers, position/argument papers.

## Traceability chain — columns

| Column | What goes here |
|---|---|
| Claim / proposition | Each core claim or proposition, page-anchored, coded D/I. |
| Premise / mechanism | The premises, prior results, or causal mechanism offered to support the claim. |
| Logical validity | Whether the argument is sound: are premises true/plausible, and does the conclusion follow? |
| Conclusion support | Whether the conclusion is actually carried by its premises, or overreaches. |

## What to assess

- **Soundness vs validity.** A valid argument has a conclusion that follows from its premises; a
  sound one also has true premises. Check both. A logically valid chain built on an unstated or
  false premise still fails.
- **Hidden premises.** The most common defect: a step that only works because of an unstated
  assumption. Surfacing it is the equivalent of a `U` (unmentioned) issue.
- **Boundary conditions / scope.** Under what conditions does the theory hold? A theory with no
  stated boundary conditions either overclaims universality or is untestable.
- **Falsifiability / testability.** Does the theory generate claims that could in principle be
  tested or refuted? If not, say so — it may be framing rather than theory.
- **Construct clarity.** Are the central concepts defined precisely enough to be used
  consistently, or do they shift meaning across the argument?
- **Novelty vs prior work.** Is the contribution a genuine advance, a relabeling, or a synthesis?
  Locate it against the cited literature.

## Type-specific failure modes (map to Gaps A/B/C)

- **Gap A (orphan):** a proposition asserted with no supporting premise or mechanism.
- **Gap B (mismeasured / unsupported):** a premise that is itself unargued or rests on a
  mischaracterized prior result; a construct too vague to support the claim built on it.
- **Gap C (overreach):** a conclusion broader than its premises license — universal claims from
  bounded premises, or no stated boundary conditions where the argument needs them.
